Putting Video on my 21 Year Old Keyboard

2026-08-15 •

Background

Four years ago, I found out about a weird set of speakers released in 2006 by Logitech called the Z10. The Z10 speakers were a set of traditional PC speakers with a integrated media controller (think play, pause, skip, etc.) and a small display for showing what was playing. The Z10 was not a very long lasting product, and most specimen have died and been thrown away many years ago. The cool part of it though, was the in built display, which had this bright orange backlight and was pretty cool for it’s time. It only worked on Windows of course, as many things did and still do, but I really liked the aesthetic it had.

Knowing this, I made a ebay saved search and kind of moved on with my life. Then, around two years later, this video by F4mi popped up on my YouTube homepage and mentioned the Z10. More importantly though, it mentioned another Logitech product with a screen, the Logitech G15 gaming keyboard.

Now, I am very familiar with keyboards, so I decided that I would buy one. Bad decision? Sure. But the plan was that I wanted to find a way to put whatever I wanted on the display. At this time, I was on Windows, so I had access to the official driver from 2005 and a lot of information about how it worked and how to make custom screens. Long story short, it sucked to use. It was designed around a ton of abstracted Windows specific libraries and made for dynamic use, like displays you could interact with via the arrow keys on the keyboard. It was really designed like the media player and while some cool stuff could be done with it, it was rough to try to program with – especially since I didn’t really know Windows application programming.

My idea at the time, was that I wanted to make a Python API for creating whatever you wanted on the screen which used the original C# libraries, but since those would need to be decompiled, the library would just be loaded into Python and used with the help of some library. This is a bad idea to be honest. I really didn’t know what I was getting myself into. I hadn’t had any experience with making drivers or low level USB at that point. I also wasn’t really prepared for how much work it would be to try and decompile the library itself and get something working that makes functional sense for a user. It was doomed to fail, so I let it, and moved on with making other cool things.

That was until I was sitting in the shower recently, thinking about another project I am working on which has to do with HID (no spoilers) and I thought back to this project. It didn’t really occur to me before this, but why did I even need to use the original driver at all? I was a year into using Linux as a daily driver at this point, and I didn’t even have access to the driver, so why was it even necessary? There are other projects (g15daemon) which do this exactly, though they are long unsupported. Why couldn’t I just refactor the code for that for USB to do whatever I wanted?

By this point in my life, I have made two keyboards and a number of projects with displays and ways to interface with humans. I have a pretty good sense of what I like and don’t like when it comes to how I use a computer and what makes me happy. When use a computer, I don’t really like having information in places other than on the screen. I like physical knobs and buttons as well as aesthetic things (the Model-K and Model-B both have displays and knobs that just exist for aesthetic). I don’t like having to look away for a specific piece of information or having to move my hands away from the keyboard area or mouse to go through menus. In all honesty, the Logitech G15 in it’s original state doesn’t make sense for what I like. It would really be better if it could just display whatever I want or playback a video. That way I could put whatever I want there and while it’s not functional, it’s certainly really cool. Sure the screen is only 160x43 pixels, but it would still be cool to have full control of it.

Making the display do what I want

It turns out, what I was thinking was actually really easy. G15daemon had a lot of the code I needed to start. I ended up using libusb, which was much easier to learn then I thought it would be, to abstract out transaction starting and closing functions, as well as making and sending a packet to the display similar to what g15daemon had. Then, using some of my experience from working with the official Windows driver, I just tried to get a bitmap image on it.

I think it’s about time I discuss the limitations of the hardware I am discussing today. 2005 was 21 years ago at the time of writing this. The display was meant for information. That means is is 1-bit color (flack or white, no grey) and 160x43. For context, 1-bit color has basically no detail, and 160x43 is so small, that 535 of the display could fit on the screen I am looking at right now. That is bad by todays standards. But there are a couple graces that it offers: it is a direct line over USB, writing to the display can be done only to the display (without effecting the rest of the keyboard), and has virtually no delay (other than being USB 1.1 from 2005). This means that I don’t really have to worry about the media keys and everything else. And even better, because it writes directly to the screen in the form of the framebuffer, I can just send a single packet of binary with a 32-byte header to the display with all of the image information I want presented, and it will just do it. No questions asked.

A bitmap is by far the easiest way to do this. Bitmaps allow you to index the colors, so low that a 0 or a 1 can be white or black. In the early days of images on computers, this was how images were typically stored. For my purposes, that makes a great starting point. I made a couple of images in GIMP which were 160x43 with 1-bit color and my goal was to send them to the display. G15daemon also had a bit of information about how this was done and so did the original implementation in the official SDK.

So it worked. The project is effectively done. I put a custom image on the display and made a program which takes in a single bitmap file and writes it. Even better, the program only has to run once and the display maintains the image. That’s better then the official driver since it was expecting dynamic interfaces.

But hold on, what’s the difference between a bitmap something like a JPEG? Well it turns out, if you use a library like stb_image, not much. It even handles PNGs. The only issue is that the sizing be correct and there are too many colors. But since it’s in a vector the same as the bitmap returns, if we just threshold the colors to make them binary and either zoom or stretch or letterbox the image, you basically have the same thing. So adding complex image support is really just as simple as adding a couple of flags and handling logic to take a image and put it on a smaller canvas. Even better than that, we can get a bit more detail by adding some dithering.

And just like that we now have a working image handler for putting whatever you want on the display.

So that was it. No post on the website. I did this long term personal goal. Got a couple of cool pictures with some memes on it. Good stuff. Time for a nap.

But wouldn’t it be cool if I could play Bad Apple!! on it

Here I am, about to fall asleep. I’m thinking about the stupid FFMPEG library and how complicated it would be to implement into this code. At this point, I’m glad it’s over and I have washed my hands of it. I have no desire to learn another library, I spent too much time trying to learn C image processing and USB. But at the same time, I’m thinking man, it would be so cool if I could play bad apple on it. But I would have to do some nonsense like I did with the Serial OLED project all those years ago where I split the frames in advance before sending them out. It would be a mess. Nobody but me would want to use it.

And then the tiny devil on my shoulder whispered in my ear, “But what if you just fork the program to the standalone version of FFMPEG and process the video there.”

No. No, that won’t work. That is stupid, why would I not use the provided library? They made it for a reason.

“But it would be really easy,” it cried. “You could just send the data back through a file descriptor. Think back to your systems programming class in college.”

At this point I was wide awake. As stupid as this was, I thought it might just work. I did some searching for examples and found something similar. I then refactored it so it would output the correct frame format and use dithering and all of that, fully expecting nothing to work and I could go to sleep. There were too many variables I changed. Heck, I was locking the frame rate, it was just going to seg fault or something. There was no way this was going to work.

But to my horror, it worked first try.

Scope Creep

After some clean up and a super dirty fix for getting the frame rate of the source video (literally the same thing but with ffprobe), I was now complete. I put the The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring on the display for a laugh and that was it. What a good project. The only thing that could make it better would be if I could game on it. Did you see that video of Bringus Studios playing games on a receipt printer? That would be crazy.

But wait, can’t FFMPEG also do camera streams? And can’t you make a virtual camera through OBS?

It was 6 lines of code.

The Result

At this point, the project is over. g15ml-V15-Final-final_for_real_this_time-V4.md. I have other things I need to do with my life, and this has ballooned to the point where I now have to spend a day making the repository look good and a page for my website. I did some clean up and made a proper release version, which is usable enough that basically anyone can run it as long as they have FFMPEG and are on Linux.

No matter what metric you look at it, this project was a success. I met every goal and stretch goal I had. The program is functional in a way I wouldn’t be embarrassed if someone else had to use it (exaggeration). It’s overall in a really good state.

There are a couple flaws that I may fix in the future, but I am really happy that there now exists a static media loader for the G15 which doesn’t need a constantly running daemon, and that goofy stuff like movies and games can be played on a garbage display like this.

That’s all. I hope the next time I post something it is the project I am working on now. Tbh I really needed a win and this project was that.

Code is here.

Until next time.
